## Meeting Recap

The committee reviewed a preliminary plan for an immersive Lehi's Dream experience. The group favored a curved,
carefully paced route that reveals one scene at a time, using visual screens and the existing trees to create a sense
of a journey without spreading the stations too far apart.

Reusable canvas backdrops, existing artwork, projection, and lighting were discussed as alternatives to building
large permanent sets. Music, donated lights, power, a preliminary materials estimate, and a possible December site
visit also came up. The plan still needs leadership direction on scope and budget; the layout, purchases, staffing,
and dates remain unconfirmed.

## Meeting Minutes

### 1. Experience and Participant Flow

- The overall goal is to create a memorable, spiritually focused experience for youth, rather than a quick walkthrough
  where participants can see every scene in advance.
- Combining the baptism and confirmation portrayals into one station was well received as a way to simplify the route.
  The final scene plan has not been approved.
- A curved or staggered path, with canvas or other visual barriers, could keep attention on the current scene and
  conceal what is around the next turn. The group preferred creating separation through sightlines rather than adding
  unnecessary distance.
- A temple scene with a couple outside the temple was discussed as a simpler backdrop than depicting an interior.
  No final design or station assignment was made.
- A walk-through lasting about 20–25 minutes for the Covenant Path and an overall experience of roughly two hours
  were discussed as planning estimates. Group size, pacing, and the final layout still need to be tested.

### 2. Scenery and Reusable Materials

- Large painted canvases could provide temple, Christ, or other scene backdrops. A family canvas-painting activity
  was suggested, but its date, dimensions, and design are undecided.
- Several large canvases from a prior Easter event were reported to be available at the stake building, including
  existing artwork of Christ. Their location, condition, and availability should be confirmed before new artwork is
  commissioned or purchased.
- A projector and screen may be available to help trace artwork or add projected imagery. The equipment and screen
  size need to be checked.
- Portable canvas backdrops and simple frames were favored as easier to move, store, and reuse than extensive
  construction. Final scene sizes and supports depend on measuring the site.
- A temple built inside the Tree of Life was discussed but did not fit the visual direction described in the meeting.
  The group leaned toward keeping the temple imagery separate and understandable to participants.

### 3. Music, Lighting, and Technical Support

- Live instrumental music was considered for some parts of the route. Daisy offered to help explore musicians through
  an orchestra contact. Rotating performers or recorded music may be needed because the event could run for hours;
  no music plan is final.
- The group discussed requesting donated solar lights, battery-powered lights, lanterns, and tea lights. Donated
  battery-powered items should include their battery packs.
- LEDs and several smaller, quieter generators were discussed as ways to reduce power use and generator noise.
  Portable Honda generators may be available, but their allocation and the overall power plan need confirmation.
- Keeping the route compact could reduce long extension-cord runs and voltage drop. Generator placement, lighting,
  sound, and projection should be planned together after the route is measured.

### 4. Budget and Purchasing

- A preliminary list totaling about **$5,000 at regular retail prices** was mentioned. It was an early estimate, not
  an approved budget or a final cost for the project.
- Goodwill, DI, Marketplace, other discount sources, and holiday sales were suggested to reduce costs. The team
  should confirm the available budget and purchasing approval process with the Lunas before committing to purchases.
- Asking the stake for lighting donations was proposed. The committee also discussed using labeled storage bins and
  keeping receipts and progress photos as work proceeds.

### 5. Schedule, Site Visit, and Approvals

- The proposal lead expected to present the plan to the Lunas the following week and seek direction on scope and
  budget. The meeting date and resulting approvals were not confirmed in the recording.
- December 5 and December 12 were floated for a possible site visit or work session. Neither date was selected; a
  December 4 service commitment may affect availability.
- A laser measuring tool and garden stakes or flags were discussed for marking distances and sightlines. The site
  should be measured before finalizing scene dimensions, spacing, and power requirements.
- Recruiting local actors or other participants, including people near Winslow, depends on leadership approval.
  Casting should wait until the Lunas clarify who may participate.

## Key Takeaways

1. The route should feel like a journey, with each scene revealed in sequence and the spiritual purpose kept central.
2. Use a compact curved path and visual barriers to improve pacing without making the route unnecessarily long.
3. Reuse canvases, existing art, projection, and available equipment where practical; confirm what is actually
   available before buying or building.
4. The roughly $5,000 figure is only an early retail estimate. The budget and scope require leadership approval.
5. Music, lighting, generators, and projection need a coordinated plan after the site is measured.
6. The next site visit, volunteer roles, and actor approvals are still pending.

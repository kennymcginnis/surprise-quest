## Meeting Recap

The committee met to organize the Lehi's Dream experience for Quest, with the central purpose of helping youth
strengthen their testimonies of Jesus Christ through the Book of Mormon. Leadership wants an immersive, memorable
experience, but the spiritual purpose should drive the creative choices. The committee's name has changed from an
emphasis on the tree to **Lehi's Dream**, reflecting responsibility for the entire experience.

Work was divided among four subcommittees: the Covenant Path; the dream portal and discussion scrolls; the great and
spacious building and mists of darkness; and the Tree of Life and rod of iron. The planned progression moves from
modern-day covenant experiences into Lehi's dream, then concludes with tribe-level reflection rather than simply ending
the activity.

The immediate priority is to prepare basic concepts, preliminary material lists, and rough budgets for the next meeting
so leadership can review the direction before substantial work begins. The group also discussed durable construction,
transport and storage, generator-powered lighting and sound, staffing approvals, a possible October site visit, and the
need to balance station spacing with pacing.

### 3. Planned Participant Experience

**Covenant Path**

- Begin with a modern-day presentation of covenant milestones, including baptism and confirmation.
- The follow-up discussion favored intentional stops rather than a quick walk past the scenes. A scripture at each
  station would allow tribes to read together while experiencing the scene.
- Dividers or other visual separation between stations were proposed to make each stop distinct. Stations could be
  staggered on both sides of the path instead of placed in one straight line.
- Sister Luna requested multiple children for the baptism and confirmation portrayals so the same child is not
  repeatedly used throughout the evening.

**Dream portal**

- Participants would arrive at an area with seating and an archway, where an actor portraying Lehi introduces the dream
  and invites them to partake of the fruit.
- Passing through the arch would mark the transition from the modern-day Covenant Path into the dream. Amy shared visual
  concepts; the final design remains subject to review.

**Great and spacious building and mists of darkness**

- Projection is an acceptable approach. Combining projection with physical scenery, such as wood and faux rock, was
  suggested to add dimension.
- Leadership liked the reference event's lighting, music, fog, and contemporary social-media distractions. The committee
  can develop its own way to incorporate those elements.
- The nearby dry wash was discussed as a possible location for fog, lighting, and voices. The intent is an impactful
  experience, not a terrifying one.
- Fog equipment may already be planned elsewhere at Quest; availability should be checked before buying or renting more.

**Rod of iron and Tree of Life**

- A firmer rod was selected as the intended direction because Brother Luna disliked the flexibility of the reference
  event's PVC version. A sample was shown, but the transcript does not reliably identify its material.
- The route would emerge from the wooded area into an open clearing, allowing the illuminated tree to stand out without
  surrounding trees casting shadows over it.

**Closing reflection**

- Each tribe would receive a scroll with discussion prompts after completing the experience.
- The goal is a short tribe devotional that helps youth process what they experienced and learned, rather than ending
  only with refreshments.

### 4. Site Layout, Lighting, and Technical Support

- The selected area includes trees, a dry wash or ravine, and an open clearing for the tree. Existing trees may help
  support scenery, subject to the eventual construction plan.
- Route measurements were preliminary and described different portions of the site: roughly 300 feet for the
  portal-to-tree portion in the first discussion, roughly 130 yards for that portion later, about 100 yards discussed
  for the Covenant Path, and roughly 200 yards of available space between clearings. These are **not a finalized layout
  or material quantity**.
- The group wanted more separation between stations than in the reference video, without spreading the experience across
  all available space and losing pacing.
- Path-lighting ideas included spotlights, strings of lights between trees, solar lights, and lanterns suspended from
  garden hooks. Hanging lanterns were favored over ground-level lanterns that could be kicked; no final lighting system
  was selected.
- The site has no electrical service. Power, lighting, speakers, projection, and cable needs must be coordinated with
  the audiovisual team.
- Generator noise was identified as a distraction, especially near the tree. Quieter generators, LED lighting to reduce
  demand, and placement that reduces audible noise were discussed; no final power plan was established.
- Two projectors and several generators were mentioned as potentially available resources. Suitability and allocation
  still need coordination.
- Other support teams include construction, logistics, security, casting, costumes, and food. Transport could involve
  committee trailers, advance loads, and the logistics team.

### 5. Budget, Purchasing, and Long-Term Use

## Key Takeaways

1. **The testimony-building purpose comes first.** The sets, lighting, actors, and sound should support youth connecting
   with Jesus Christ and the Book of Mormon.
2. **This is a complete experience, not just a tree display.** The Covenant Path, portal, dream, and closing reflection
   need to work together.
3. **Bring an initial concept, not a finished production, to the next meeting.** Each team needs a basic plan, material
   list, and rough budget so leadership can approve the direction early.
4. **Immersive does not mean unlimited spending.** No committee allocation was announced; purchases and concepts still
   require approval.
5. **Build for repeated use.** Construction choices must account for setup, teardown, transport, heat, and storage over
   multiple event cycles.
6. **Pacing and infrastructure are central planning constraints.** Station spacing, power, generator noise, path
   lighting, and next-day teardown need coordinated solutions.
7. **There is help available, with attendance limits.** Preparation volunteers are welcome, but camp staffing and
   additional actors must follow the approval process.
